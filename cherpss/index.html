    A LITTLE CHESS. A LITTLE CHAOS.

    How to play

    01 · The board

    Take turns clicking or tapping pieces. Move hints can be switched on or off; legal-move rules always apply. Pieces move as in chess, including castling, en passant and promotion. In Duel Chess there is no check or checkmate: you can challenge a king. Defeat the enemy king or exhaust their three champions to win.

    02 · The challenge

    A capture opens a duel. Pick privately: Ivory presses F / G / H for Rock / Scissors / Paper; Ember presses J / K / L. Controller choices: A / X / B. On a phone, pass the device between private picks while the other player looks away. Both champions reveal together.

    03 · The arena

    Manual battles: move to face a direction, hold attack to repeat, hold guard to protect, and use your special when ready. The Rock hits hardest but winds up first: a red marker shows where its swing or charge will land, so step aside. Phones start with automatic battles: both champions fight for you. You can switch battle controls on the main screen. Dark squares are night; light squares are day.

    04 · Timing & wounds

    Choose no time limit to fight until a knockout, or a one-minute limit. With no time limit, a duel still going after one minute turns into an attacker surge: the attacker keeps getting faster, longer-reaching and harder-hitting (twice as strong at two minutes, four times at three). If the minute expires, the attacker wins; damage still leaves persistent wounds. The losing piece disappears; a double knockout removes both. A losing champion loses 40 vitality; the winner carries a small wound (up to 20). At zero, a champion is unavailable.

    05 · Your neighbours

    The target square and its eight neighbours support their side; the attacker also joins. Pawns add a little shield and edge, knights add speed, bishops make specials recharge faster and hit harder, rooks power up specials, queens speed up attacks, kings add damage. Bonuses have limits.

    06 · One last challenge?

    After roster defeat, the loser can request ordinary chess. The winner can accept or keep the win. Both kings must remain and the position must be valid. Classic mode uses king safety, checkmate and draw rules; combat is then finished.

    Prototype rules and balance. Sounds start after your first tap or key press and can be muted. Online lobbies are a future idea. Duels cannot be paused, and reloading the page resumes a duel where it was.
